\paragraph{NACA~0012 Comparison}
Because the low-Reynolds-number data of Kurtulus~\cite{Kurtulus2022criticalangle} are for the NACA~0012 rather than the NACA~0008, we repeated the sweep for the NACA~0012 with the same set-up: $M_\infty = 0.2$, $\mathrm{Re} = 2000$, a $32c\times24c$ domain with characteristic inflow and outflow boundaries on all four sides, five levels of refinement (finest $\Delta x = c/512$), WENO5 reconstruction, and the airfoil embedded as a sharp no-slip wall. Kurtulus solved the incompressible laminar Navier--Stokes equations with a second-order finite-volume solver (ANSYS Fluent) on a body-fitted grid of about $2\times10^{5}$ cells with a domain extending $19c$ from the airfoil, and averaged the forces over the quasi-periodic state between $50$ and $100$~s. He reports $C_l$ and $C_d$ only in figures, except for $\alpha = 10^{\circ}$ in his Table~1, so the remaining reference values were read from his Fig.~5 and cross-checked against his Fig.~6, with a reading uncertainty of about $\pm0.007$ in $C_l$ and $\pm0.0013$ in $C_d$. At $M_\infty = 0.2$ the compressibility correction to the lift is $1/\beta = 1.02$, so we compare the coefficients directly.

The time window is the main difference between the two computations. Kurtulus's Strouhal number and fundamental frequency at the critical angle ($St = 1.22$, $f_{cr} = 3.56$~Hz) imply $U_\infty/c \approx 2.9~\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, so his averages cover roughly $tU_\infty/c \in [146, 292]$. As for the NACA~0008, we averaged over $tU_\infty/c \in [6.7, 10]$ ($t\in[33.3,50]$ in code units, $c/U_\infty = 5$), which is all that a run of about one day per angle allows at this resolution. At that time the NACA~0008 lift was still drifting by about 1\% per convective time at $\alpha = 6^{\circ}$, so the present means are early-time values and not the long-time state of the reference. The comparison in Table~\ref{tab:val_naca0012_sweep} and Figure~\ref{fig:val_naca0012_polars} should be read with that in mind.
